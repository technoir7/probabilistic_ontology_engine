# Themes report — v1

You are writing about which themes in art criticism help an artist succeed, for a
busy, intelligent reader. Write the way a good critic writes to a colleague:
plainly, specifically, in your own voice. The reader should not be able to tell a
machine helped.

Start directly with "## In short". No preamble.

## What the data is

Artists that the Art Institute of Chicago or the Cleveland Museum of Art collected
were followed year by year, with four kinds of success the year after: critics
approve, more press, museum buys, and a place in Artprice's Top 500 by auction
turnover (the market). `how_records_are_made` says how each theme gets a track
record for each kind, from earlier years only. For each kind, `leader` is what
predicts that kind best once the records are allowed in; `leader_effects` give how
much each feature changes the odds, worked out as
`times_the_odds_of_an_artist_with_the_same_past_and_none`: quote only those figures,
never work one out yourself, and never set one level against another as a ratio.
`best_records_this_year` and `worst_records_this_year` are the named themes with the
best and worst records now.

`verdicts` are conclusions fixed by rule. State each plainly in your own words;
never soften, hedge or contradict one, and draw nothing stronger than it allows.
Where a verdict says a theme's record adds nothing for a kind, its best and worst
themes are description of the past only: say so, and draw no advice from them.

## Structure

## In short
Two or three sentences: for which kinds of success a theme's record helps, and the
one thing the reader should take from it.

## Where a theme's record helps
For each kind where the verdicts say the record joins what leads: how much it helps,
which way, whether the coverage comes before the success, and the themes with the
best and worst records, with what they have in common if anything plainly does.

## Where it does not
One short paragraph on the other kinds: the record adds nothing there, so what
predicts them is the kind of theme (growing, argued over and so on), not which one.

## How far to trust it
One short paragraph: records are learned from earlier years only, and shrunk when a
theme has few cases; only artists the two museums collected; the themes come from
models reading the press and grouping and naming its claims; only named themes are
shown; the market is only the top 500 by auction turnover.

## How to write

- Up to 600 words. Short sentences. Ordinary words.
- Use only what is in the data. Do not add facts, dates, names or context from
  elsewhere, even if you know them.
- Never write a field name (anything with underscores) or mention the data, the
  system, the model, the verdicts or these instructions.
- No bullet points in the report itself.

Never use these words or moves: delve, landscape, tapestry, nuanced, multifaceted,
underscores, highlights the importance, a testament to, paradoxically, notably,
crucially, it's worth noting, it is important to note, navigate, in conclusion,
overall, robust, "not just X but Y", "while X, Y" openings, rhetorical questions,
groups of three adjectives, and dashes used as commas.
